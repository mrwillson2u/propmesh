# PropMesh Mini — plan audit, 2026-09-16

**Assessment:** a useful product concept, particularly for a small prop that
wakes on a local sensor, plays a short effect, then sleeps. Keep the C3-class
controller, flash audio, I²S amplifier, native USB and battery-leaf role. The
plan needs a power architecture and resource-budget pass before schematic
capture. Its present sleep, output-current, storage and cost claims are not
yet supported by a complete circuit.

**Scope:** hardware-spec §3, the Mini pinmap/generated header, relevant software
plan and current firmware, plus manufacturer documentation for the proposed
MCU, regulators, charger, switching FET, IR receiver, logic and shared output
components. `hardware/mini/` contained only a README: there is no schematic or
PCB to run ERC/DRC on. Findings below describe plan contradictions and missing
design decisions, not observed failures of an implemented board. No pinmap,
firmware or hardware design changes were made by this audit.

## 1. Define the always-on and switched power domains first

**High priority.** The plan says the 5 V rail and boost turn off in deep sleep,
but does not specify the complete connections or control of boost EN, the load
switch, the battery-sense disconnect, PCA9685, Qwiic and wake sensors.

The MCU and its regulator must remain powered to wake. A PIR fed from the
switched 5 V sensor supply cannot detect motion while that supply is off. The
hardware spec describes the common HC-SR501 as a 5 V-powered sensor, so either
provide an always-on sensor supply and budget it, or choose a wake sensor that
runs from the always-on low-voltage domain. Do not assume any sensor plugged
into GPIO4 meets the sleep budget.

| Domain | What belongs there | Design question |
|---|---|---|
| Always-on | MCU/RTC, 3.3 V regulator, battery protection, selected wake sensor | Exact current across battery voltage and temperature |
| Switched effects | 5 V strip, amp, relay coil, servo power | Default-off behavior, total peak current, startup/inrush and shutdown order |
| Explicitly assigned | PCA9685 logic, Qwiic, IR, indicators, ADC divider | Retained state versus power-off leakage and recovery |

Check every signal crossing between domains: servo/PCA outputs, I²S, strip data,
sensor outputs and I²C can unintentionally power a disabled peripheral. Do not
blanket-assume every buffer back-powers: the exact Nexperia AHCT1G125 has
overvoltage-tolerant inputs, but that does not certify other connected devices
or another supplier's substitute. Use defined low/high-impedance states and
parts whose powered-off behavior is specified.

## 2. The proposed active-low rail control is incomplete electrically

**High priority.** GPIO2's pulled-high/off convention is a sensible default, but
does not itself implement a 5 V high-side switch. If GPIO2 directly drives an
AO3401 gate whose source is 5 V, a 3.3 V logic high leaves **VGS = −1.7 V**;
that is not a guaranteed off state. Pulling that GPIO itself up to 5 V is not
acceptable either. Use a proper level-shifted gate circuit or compatible load
switch, with off-state defaults during reset, power sequencing and USB operation.
[AO3401 datasheet](https://www.aosmd.com/res/data_sheets/AO3401.pdf).

The TPS61023 has **active-high EN and true input/output disconnection in
shutdown**. The plan assumes EN low in sleep without assigning the required
control/inversion. Exploit its specified disconnect behavior deliberately; a
separate FET may still be useful for domains or external-power isolation, but
its necessity must follow the complete topology. This refines the locked
requirement to disconnect strip power; it does not remove that requirement.
[TPS61023 §7.3.2](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps61023.pdf).

Also correct the explanation that GPIO2 low necessarily selects the wrong boot
mode: Espressif recommends pulling it high because of glitches, but states
that GPIO2 does not actually determine SPI versus download boot. Keep a robust
default, with the rationale grounded in the current datasheet.

## 3. The 15 µA sleep budget is incorrect for the listed parts

**Confirmed numbers, high priority.**

| Part | Datasheet information | Budget implication |
|---|---|---|
| ME6211C33 | 60 µA typical supply current in the C33 table; family headline says 40 µA. 0.1 µA is disabled standby | The MCU's always-on LDO cannot use the disabled figure; the specified 1 µA is unsupported |
| TSOP38238 | 0.35 mA typical in darkness at 3.3 V | IR wake alone exceeds the entire advertised budget by over 20× |
| PCA9685 | 2.2 µA typical standby, up to 15.5 µA under specified conditions; operating current is much larger | Explicitly command sleep and define outputs, or power it down with signal isolation |
| TPS61023 | 0.1 µA typical shutdown input current | Only applies when EN is actually low; an output FET alone does not shut down the converter |
| Protection, charger, wake sensor, pull networks, indicators and attached Qwiic devices | Not included in the table | Measure and budget each; accessory current is not bounded by the MCU sleep specification |

Sources: [Microne ME6211 V14, C33 table p8](https://wmsc.lcsc.com/wmsc/upload/file/pdf/v2/lcsc/1811131510_MICRONE-Nanjing-Micro-One-Elec-ME6211C33M5G-N_C82942.pdf),
[Vishay TSOP382/384](https://www.vishay.com/docs/82491/tsop382.pdf),
[NXP PCA9685 Table 14](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/PCA9685.pdf),
[TI TPS61023](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps61023.pdf).

ME6211C33 plus an always-on IR receiver already total about **410 µA typical**,
before the MCU, PIR and other circuitry. Months of light-duty operation may
still be possible, but not from the stated 15 µA calculation. Consider dropping
onboard IR as already suggested in the pinmap; this saves current and a valuable
wake-capable GPIO. An external IR receiver must still be powered and budgeted.

## 4. Choose the 3.3 V supply and battery cutoff together

**High priority design gap.** If the ME6211 is fed directly from a 1S cell, it
cannot maintain 3.3 V over the full discharge range. Its C33 table lists typical
dropout of 120 mV at 100 mA and 260 mV at 200 mA. The C3 module requires
3.0–3.6 V and has radio current bursts; sag and dropout can cause resets before
the cell is depleted.

If the LDO instead runs from the 5 V boost, shutting that boost off also removes
the MCU's wake supply. This is why a power tree is needed before selecting the
regulator. Choose a suitable low-Iq always-on regulator and deliberate cutoff
behavior, or accept and quantify the usable capacity lost with an LDO. A
protection IC's emergency undervoltage trip is not a clean firmware shutdown
threshold. Verify the exact cell, holder, contact resistance and protection
FETs at low battery and radio/servo load transients.

## 5. The 5 V output is not a 3 A supply, and 1.2 A is not valid everywhere

**Confirmed rating/budget problem.** The TPS61023's 3.7 A figure is a switching
current limit, not a 5 V output rating. For the proposed 1.2 A output:

`I_battery ≈ (5 V × 1.2 A) / (V_battery × efficiency)`

| Battery voltage | Battery current at assumed 90% efficiency, before MCU/other loads |
|---|---:|
| 4.2 V | 1.59 A |
| 3.6 V | 1.85 A |
| 3.0 V | **2.22 A** |

The [JST PH rating is 2 A with AWG24 wire](https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/eng/ePH.pdf).
At 3.0 V, even the idealized 2 A input budget allows only 1.08 A at 5 V before
other loads. The spec's “1.5 W from the cell per watt delivered” is not the
correct general relationship: power scales by 1/efficiency; **current** also
scales by the voltage ratio.

Rate the complete chain at its worst operating point: cell, holder, connector,
protection pair, load-share FET, inductor, boost thermals, copper and output
connector. The generic “1 µH” BOM line needs saturation/RMS current and DCR
requirements. Three servos, a speaker and a strip share the same budget; LED
count alone cannot enforce it. Define per-port and total limits and test stalls
and simultaneous startup. These calculations do not select a final rating.

## 6. Audio storage conflicts with the planned OTA size

**Confirmed arithmetic conflict.** `docs/software-plan.md` reserves roughly
2 MB of the 4 MB N4 flash for audio and discusses a roughly 1.5 MB OTA image.
Normal ESP-IDF application OTA needs two firmware slots plus OTA metadata.
Two 1.5 MB slots plus 2 MB audio already need **5 MB**, excluding bootloader,
NVS and partition metadata.
[Espressif OTA documentation](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/system/ota.html).

Create a real partition table and build-size budget before locking the module.
An illustrative 4 MiB arrangement with two 1.5 MiB slots and 128 KiB reserved
for other needs leaves 0.875 MiB for files: at 64 kbit/s, that is only about
115 seconds of audio before filesystem overhead and sequences/configuration.
This is an example, not a proposed production partition table.

Short compressed clips still make sense. If approximately 2 MB of audio and
robust OTA are both requirements, choose sufficient flash now. Do not assume
an 8 MB external-antenna version of the chosen module exists: the current
datasheet lists an H8X for the PCB-antenna MINI-1, but not for MINI-1U.
MP3 playback plus mesh, LED timing and file access also needs a C3 memory/CPU
prototype; the current repository implements a mesh testbed, not that complete
workload or its wake-to-audio latency.

## 7. Servo and LED PWM cannot have independent frequencies on one PCA9685

**Confirmed conflict.** The Mini uses channels 0–2 for servos and 3–6 for LED
dimming. `pinmap.yaml` defines `SERVO_PWM_HZ = 50` and `LED_PWM_HZ = 1000`,
but the PCA9685 has one shared frequency. Additional unused channels do not
provide additional timers.

Either accept 50 Hz dimming and its possible visible/camera flicker, or add an
independent PWM resource. Keep the PCA for servos if desired; this finding
qualifies the original multifunction choice. Also define hardware output
disable during MCU reset and power transitions: a still-powered PCA retains
programmed state through an MCU-only reset.
[PCA9685 general description and PRE_SCALE](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/PCA9685.pdf).

## 8. Charging and solar need a specified power path

**High priority design gap.** TP4056 plus AO3401/Schottky load sharing can be
reasonable, but the schematic must distinguish the battery charge path from
system load, handle both USB and solar, and prevent backfeed into either input.
If the system load is charged through the BAT node, it can interfere with
charge termination. If external input feeds the system, charge current plus
system current must fit that source.

A 6 V/1 W panel supplies only about **167 mA at its rated operating point**;
the charger cannot be configured as though it has a stiff 1 A source. Panel
open-circuit voltage, low-light behavior and temperature need to be specified.
If a solar-fed load-share node also feeds the TPS61023, keep it within that
device's 5.5 V input operating limit; a single Schottky is not a voltage regulator.

The [TP4056 manufacturer datasheet](https://www.toppwr.com/uploadfile/file/20230304/640301eae1260.pdf)
includes thermal regulation and a battery-temperature input. At 5 V input,
3 V battery and 1 A charge current, a linear charger would dissipate roughly
2 W before throttling. Set charge current for the actual source, cell and copper.
Specify a battery NTC/temperature inhibit for the intended outdoor conditions;
charger die thermal limiting does not measure cell temperature. A two-wire
JST-PH battery connector alone provides no cell-temperature measurement.

The proposed [CN3065 alternative](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/Solar_Charger_Shield_V2.2/res/DSE-CN3065.pdf)
does adapt charging to a current-limited source, but its operating input range
ends at 6 V and absolute maximum at 6.5 V. A nominal 6 V panel is not
automatically compatible. It is still a linear charger, not a lossless converter
or a promise of 5 Wh delivered into the battery.

## 9. Recalculate runtime and solar with explicit assumptions

The 3,000 mAh × 3.6 V = **10.8 Wh** figure is nominal stored energy, not a
verified usable output-energy figure. Applying an illustrative 90% conversion
factor to the current load estimates changes 5.87/13.5 hours to about
**5.3/12.2 hours**. If only 85% of nameplate energy is available before cutoff,
those examples become **4.5/10.3 hours**. These are sensitivity calculations,
not measured runtimes; load-dependent conversion, temperature, cell age and
voltage sag determine the actual values.

For trigger mode, specify events per day and effect duration. As an example,
100 events/day × 10 seconds × 2 W costs **0.56 Wh/day**, before idle and
conversion losses. Even zero sleep current would then exhaust a nominal
10.8 Wh cell in under 20 days. “Months” can describe infrequent effects, not
every trigger-mode workload.

Similarly, 1 W × five equivalent full-sun hours is **5 Wh at the panel**, before
orientation, shade, wiring, charger and storage losses. Define a daily energy
budget and local assumptions before claiming solar sustains the prop. No
weather/location-specific yield was estimated in this audit.

## 10. The GPIO map closes, but control details and module selection remain

The map assigns **15 unique, valid exposed GPIO**, not “13 usable” as one
paragraph states. The generated header matches; `python3 tools/genpins.py
--check` passes. GPIO4 is a suitable deep-sleep wake choice; GPIO3 is too.
I²S, USB and ADC allocations are plausible. ADC inputs are ADC1.

The [current module datasheet v2.2](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-c3-mini-1_datasheet_en.pdf)
marks **ESP32-C3-MINI-1U-N4 as NRND** and recommends **N4X/H4X**. Use the current
part for new sourcing, subject to the flash decision above. Follow the
[chip revision v1.1 compatibility advisory](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/advisory_downloads/ar2024-009_en.pdf)
when choosing firmware/toolchain settings; do not merely rename the BOM.

Remaining details:

- The battery-sense disconnect FET has no allocated control. A spare PCA output
  could control it with correct defaults, or quantify whether the leakage is
  worth switching at all. A 1 MΩ/1 MΩ divider draws 2.1 µA at 4.2 V; with 100 nF
  at the ADC its time constant is 50 ms, so allow settling after connection.
- Specify status-LED type/control. There is no spare MCU status-pixel pin.
  A simple LED on a spare PCA output or switched rail avoids inventing one.
- GPIO8's I²C pull-up is reasonable, but attached devices can hold SDA low;
  GPIO8 must be high for download boot with GPIO9 low. Preserve a recovery path.
- The GPIO21 series resistor limits contention with ROM TX output; it does not
  make the input 5 V tolerant. Include the actual input protection circuit.

The [C3 sleep documentation](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/system/sleep_modes.html)
confirms the radio is off in deep sleep. The software plan correctly treats
battery Minis as leaves. A sleeping Mini cannot receive an immediate mesh cue;
retain explicit local-trigger and continuously-listening modes. IR activity can
wake the chip, but the first command may be lost while it boots; do not promise
first-frame decode without measurement and a suitable repeat/wake interaction.

## 11. The BOM and 50 × 40 mm outline are incomplete promises

The listed BOM adds correctly to **$11.17**, but four dimmable load channels
have no explicit power FETs/drivers, flybacks where needed, or output connectors.
The PCA cannot directly supply arbitrary prop lighting. The battery-sense
switch/control, complete load-switch drive, cell-temperature support and final
support passives also need explicit parts. Consequently the landed-cost/retail
margin calculation is premature; this audit did not refresh supplier prices,
tariffs or assembly quotes.

The small outline is plausible only after an actual connector placement test.
Include all sensor **three-pin** connectors, four DC outputs, speaker, strip,
relay, solar, battery, three servo headers, USB, Qwiic and antenna-cable access.
Check screwdriver and plug clearance and the relay's separation from logic.
This is an area/access risk, not proof that 50 × 40 mm cannot work.

Two layers are a reasonable target for this feature set if the boost hot loop
and return paths are compact and a useful ground reference survives routing.
Keep input/output boost ceramics at their IC pins, select the inductor for peak
current, keep switch copper away from ADC/audio/RF, and reserve copper for the
charger and amp. Placement should begin with connectors and those power blocks,
not with packing the MCU into the remaining gap.

## 12. Mesh access while saving power — follow-up requirement

The Mini can remain logically reachable with a bounded delay while the radio
sleeps between listening windows. It cannot receive an arbitrary Wi-Fi/ESP-NOW
packet during deep sleep. This adds a useful middle mode between sensor-only
sleep and continuously listening.

Espressif supports ESP-NOW receive windows in station mode using
`esp_now_set_wake_window()` and an interval API (named
`esp_wifi_connectionless_module_set_wake_interval()` in the ESP-NOW API guide).
For this project's unassociated stations, enable/verify
`CONFIG_ESP_WIFI_STA_DISCONNECTED_PM_ENABLE`. Combine radio power saving with
appropriate CPU power management/automatic light sleep where supported by the
chosen build. Merely setting a radio window does not establish a low total-board
current. Confirm API/configuration availability in the actual pinned Arduino/IDF
build before implementation.
Sources: [ESP-NOW power saving](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_now.html#config-esp-now-power-saving-parameter)
and [connectionless Wi-Fi power saving](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-guides/wifi-driver/wifi-performance-and-power-save.html#connectionless-modules-power-saving).

Suggested product modes; intervals below are starting design targets, not
measured latency or energy guarantees:

| Mode | Behavior | Remote-access consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Show / editing | Radio stays available; effects rail only as needed | Prompt commands, uploads and live control; greatest idle energy |
| Reachable standby | Periodic receive/poll windows, initially target one every 0.5–1 second | Normal contact within roughly one interval plus mesh/retry latency; higher consumption than deep sleep |
| Long standby | Deep sleep; GPIO wake plus timer check-in, initially 30–60 seconds | Remote command waits until check-in, then the node can remain awake for a session |

Keep the powered Pro/bridge available. When the user opens a Mini in the console,
queue a request to keep it awake. On its next check-in, the Mini receives that
request, acknowledges it and stays listening for a renewable session timeout.
Close the session or let the timeout expire to return to standby. A local sensor
can wake it immediately regardless of the check-in timer. This provides remote
maintenance without walking to a button, with an explicit delay/power tradeoff.

The existing mesh is a broadcast flood with duplicate suppression and short
relay jitter; it does not yet implement a mailbox for sleeping leaves. Add
poll/reply or coordinated retransmission, acknowledgements, request expiry and
command IDs that prevent duplicate effects. In multi-hop operation the reply
must survive to a powered neighbor of the Mini; retries must also account for
the flood's sequence-number duplicate filter. Distinguish sleeping from missing
in the UI using the advertised next check-in.

For a timed show, deliver the cue and wake schedule in advance, or keep the
node listening for the show. Do not replay an expired fire command seconds
later. Reacquire mesh time after deep sleep before acting on absolute timestamps.
The phone still talks to the powered Pro's console; the Mini need not keep its
own access point or web server running.

Regular Wi-Fi associated with a router has its own modem/automatic-light-sleep
mechanisms, but AP buffering is not an automatic delivery guarantee for this
unassociated ESP-NOW flood. Use the connectionless design above rather than
assuming ordinary Wi-Fi power saving solves mesh delivery.

## Recommended next design pass

1. Freeze the product modes and supported loads: trigger, periodic mesh access or listening,
   exact wake sensor, battery cutoff, shared 5 V limits and external-load power.
2. Draw the power tree and rail-control circuit, including USB/solar coexistence,
   charging, temperature sensing and all off-state signal paths.
3. Choose the current MCU module and real flash partitions; decide how to handle
   the PCA's shared PWM rate. Keep onboard IR only if it earns its current/pin.
4. Complete the load-driver/connector BOM, then perform a 50 × 40 mm placement
   trial and revise the size/cost from that evidence.
5. Prototype sleep current, cold/low-battery wake, boost startup with real loads,
   charging under load and shade, and MP3+mesh+LED operation before ordering PCB.

The Mini is worth pursuing. Its strongest differentiator is a compact,
sensor-triggered battery prop with short local audio and modest lighting.
Retaining every output is possible in principle, but their combined power and
connector cost must be explicit rather than hidden in a “small Pro” assumption.
