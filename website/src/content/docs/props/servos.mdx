A hobby servo turns to an angle and holds it. It is the classic choice for a skull jaw, a turning head, or a lid that lifts.

## Wiring

A servo has three wires:

| Servo wire | Goes to |
|---|---|
| **brown / black** (ground) | the servo supply's GND **and** a board GND |
| **red** (power) | a separate **5 to 6 V supply** |
| **orange / yellow / white** (signal) | a board pin |

<Aside type="caution" title="Give servos their own power">
Don't power a servo from the board's VIN or 3V3 pins. When a servo starts to move it pulls a burst of current, the board's voltage dips, and the board restarts, usually in the middle of a scare. The fix is a 5 V supply of 2 A or more, with its ground joined to the board's.
</Aside>

**Which pin?** The board pages mark the best servo pins with ★. Those pins stay quiet while the board starts up, so the prop doesn't twitch every time it gets power.

## Setting it up in the console

Assign the pin to **Servo**. Its options are:

- **min** / **max (µs)**: the pulse lengths at each end of travel. The defaults are 500 and 2500. Most props only want part of the servo's range. A jaw might only need to open a little.
- **reverse**: swaps the direction.
- **smoothing**: how the servo gets from one position to the next. Choose **Off** (it snaps), **Snappy · 250 ms**, **Smooth · 600 ms**, **Gentle · 1000 ms**, **Slow · 1500 ms**, or **Custom…**.

### Finding the end stops

After **Save to node**, press **Calibrate** in the TEST column. Move the slider until the jaw is just closed and press **Set as min**. Move it until the jaw is open as far as it should go and press **Set as max**. Then press **Done** and save. From now on, 0 % means "closed" and 100 % means "open".

<Aside type="tip">Start from the middle and move toward each end slowly. A servo pushed past the prop's real limit buzzes and gets hot.</Aside>
