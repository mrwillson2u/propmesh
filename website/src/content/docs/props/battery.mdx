A prop in the middle of the lawn with no extension cord: a board on a battery can do that. It sleeps when the show is off and wakes when it's needed.

## The board

The **[Adafruit HUZZAH32 Feather](/propmesh/boards/esp32-huzzah32/)** has a LiPo battery connector and charger built in, and reports its battery voltage. The **[ESP32-C6 SuperMini](/propmesh/boards/esp32-c6-supermini/)** sleeps on very little power but needs an external charger and regulator.

## Turning it on

Open the board in **Nodes & Channels**, switch on **On battery**, and save. Then the board:

- **becomes a leaf** — it never passes messages along for other boards (they can't count on it being awake);
- **listens in short windows while the show runs** — by default 100 ms of every second, so a cue reaches it within about a second;
- **deep-sleeps when the show is off**, checking in every few minutes, and wakes on its own for the next schedule window.

The **SLEEP** settings on the board's page change those numbers and show the average current each choice costs.

## Sensors on a battery board

A sleeping board wakes the moment its sensor fires — but only from certain pins. The board pages mark them (**wakes from sleep**), and the console warns you if a sensor is on a pin that can't wake the board.

<Aside type="caution" title="What a battery board is bad at">
Anything that needs it awake: it reacts to cues up to a second late while the show runs, and not at all while asleep. Use it for props that do their own thing (a sensor and a light on the same board), and keep timing-critical props on mains power.
</Aside>

In the Yard, a sleeping board shows **☾ asleep** and its battery voltage — amber when it's getting low (under 3.5 V).
