A prop in the middle of the lawn with no extension cord is possible with a board on a battery. The board sleeps when the show is off and wakes when it is needed.

## The board

The **[Adafruit HUZZAH32 Feather](/propmesh/boards/esp32-huzzah32/)** has a LiPo battery connector and charger built in, and it reports its battery voltage. The **[ESP32-C6 SuperMini](/propmesh/boards/esp32-c6-supermini/)** and **[ESP32-C3 SuperMini](/propmesh/boards/esp32-c3-supermini/)** sleep on very little power but need an external charger and regulator (some C6 versions have charger pads; see the board page).

<Aside type="note" title="How far this has been tested">
Battery sleep has run on the project's Feather on the bench: deep sleep, timer wake, and cues reaching a board that listens in windows. A full night in the yard on a battery hasn't happened yet, and neither SuperMini has been flashed at all. Expect to tune the numbers below.
</Aside>

## Turning it on

Open the board in **Nodes & Channels**, switch on **On battery**, and save. From then on the board:

- **becomes a leaf.** It never passes messages along for other boards, because they can't count on it being awake.
- **listens in short windows while the show runs.** By default it listens for 100 ms out of every second. The other boards know this and send cues early enough to land in a window, so a battery board fires together with everyone else.
- **deep-sleeps when the show is off.** It checks in every five minutes by default, and wakes on its own in time for the next schedule window.

The **SLEEP** settings on the board's page let you change those numbers, and show roughly what each choice costs in average current.

## Sensors on a battery board

A sleeping board wakes the moment its own sensor fires, but only from certain pins. The board pages mark them as **wakes from sleep**, and the console warns you if a sensor is on a pin that can't wake the board.

<Aside type="caution" title="What a battery board is bad at">
Anything that needs it awake. While the show runs, a scare that involves a battery board starts about a second after the sensor fires instead of almost at once, because the cue has to wait for the board's next listen window. While the show is off, it only wakes for its own sensor. Use it for props that do their own thing, such as a sensor and a light on the same board, and keep props with tight timing on mains power.
</Aside>

In the Yard, a sleeping board shows **☾ asleep** and its battery voltage. The voltage turns amber when it is getting low (under 3.5 V).
