Many flameless candles have a plastic flame on a pivot, lit from below by a single LED, with a small coil underneath that nudges the flame so it sways. With the candle's own chip removed, a PropMesh board can drive the LED and the coil, so the candle flickers, sways and sputters on cue, and goes dark when the show does.

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet tested on a real candle">
The candle output is new. Its flicker comes from a sketch that ran on a real candle, but the PropMesh version has only run in the console's simulator and in automated tests. Please report how it looks.
</Aside>

## Wiring

You need two pins: one for the LED and one for the coil.

- **The LED.** Most candle boards feed the LED from the battery through a resistor and switch its other leg to ground. Connect that leg to a board pin, and tick **LED on = LOW** in the console. If instead the pin feeds the LED directly (pin → resistor → LED → ground), leave it unticked.
- **The coil.** Never connect the coil straight to a pin. The candle board drives it through a transistor; connect a board pin to the transistor's input, usually through the resistor that is already there.
- **Power.** The candle's battery can power the board through its 5V or VIN pin. The slide switch then turns everything off.

<Aside type="tip" title="Check before you power it">
If the coil's transistor has no resistor from its input to ground, the coil can twitch while the board starts up. A 10 kΩ resistor from the transistor's input to ground keeps it still.
</Aside>

## Setting it up in the console

Assign the LED's pin to **Candle (flicker LED + flame coil)**, then choose the **coil** pin beside it. The **CANDLE** settings shape the flame:

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **drama** | how strong every effect is: 0 is a calm, steady flame, 1 is normal, 1.5 is wild |
| **floor** | the dimmest the flame ever gets, even as it nearly blows out |
| **gust every** | the average time between gusts: quick dips with a big swing of the flame |
| **sputter every** | the average time between near-blowouts, where the flame stutters and flares back. 0 turns them off. |
| **swing** | how often the coil kicks. Match it to the flame's own swing. |
| **kick** | how long each coil pulse lasts. Longer kicks swing harder and use more battery. |
| **swing the flame** | untick to keep the flame still while the LED flickers |

Gusts and sputters come at random intervals around those averages, so the flicker never falls into a rhythm.

### Finding the swing

Each candle's flame piece has its own natural swing, like a pendulum, and the coil moves it furthest when its kicks land at that rate. Press **Find the swing…** in the candle's TEST column. The board then kicks the coil steadily at one rate, with no rests and no gusts, so all you have to judge is how far the flame swings.

- **Sweep** steps the rate up by 0.10 Hz every 2.5 seconds, from 1 Hz to 5 Hz and around again. Watch the flame and note the rate where it swings widest. The sweet spot is usually between 2 and 4 Hz.
- **−** and **+** nudge the rate by 0.05 Hz. Pause the sweep near the widest swing and nudge until the motion peaks.
- **Use** puts that rate into the candle's **swing** setting. Then press **Save to node**.
- **Stop** ends the kicks. The board also stops on its own after two minutes, because the coil warms under steady kicking.

Then keep **kick** as short as still gives a good swing. A coil that gets warm is being pushed too hard.

## In the show

The candle's **TEST** column has a brightness slider, **Light** and **Out**, and **Gust** and **Sputter** buttons to try the effects on demand.

In a sequence, a candle is a track like an LED dimmer: its value is the brightness of the flicker, 0 puts it out, and it can fade. A candle that fades up at dusk as part of an ambient loop, and gusts when a scare goes off nearby, is a few steps.
