Addressable LED strips are the fastest way to make a prop look alive: glowing eyes, a lantern that flickers, a cauldron that bubbles green.

## Which strip?

| Strip | Wires to the board | Console function |
|---|---|---|
| **WS2812B** (often sold as "NeoPixel") | one data wire | **LED strip (WS2812)** |
| **SK9822** or **APA102** ("DotStar") | data **and** clock | **LED strip (SK9822 / APA102, clocked)** |

Either is fine. WS2812 is cheaper and everywhere; the clocked kind is more forgiving about timing and long wires. Each board page lists how many strips it can run (the DevKit V1: up to 8 WS2812 strips).

## Wiring

```
Power supply 5V ─────────────── strip 5V
Power supply GND ──┬─────────── strip GND
                   └─────────── board GND
Board pin ── level shifter ──── strip DIN  (and CLK for a clocked strip)
```

1. **Power the strip from its own 5 V supply**, not from the board. A pixel at full white draws about 60 mA, so 30 pixels can want nearly 2 A — far more than a USB port or the board's regulator can give.
2. **Join the grounds.** The supply's GND, the strip's GND and the board's GND all connect. A strip that flickers randomly is very often a missing ground.
3. **Use a level shifter on the data line.** The board speaks 3.3 V; a 5 V strip expects close to 5 V. It often works without one — until it's cold, or the wire is long, and then it flickers. A **74AHCT125** (or the one-channel **74AHCT1G125**) fixes it for under a dollar.

<Aside type="tip" title="The small extras that prevent headaches">
A **330 Ω resistor** in series with the data line, close to the strip, and a **1000 µF capacitor** across 5 V and GND where power enters the strip. Both are cheap insurance.
</Aside>

## Setting it up in the console

Assign the pin to **LED strip (WS2812)** and set:

- **pixels** — how many LEDs on the strip (up to 300).
- **order** — the color order: try **GRB** first (most WS2812). If red shows as green, switch to **RGB**.
- **effect** — **solid color** or **fire**.

**Save to node**, then use the color picker in the TEST column. A solid strip takes any color in a sequence; **Off** turns it dark.

## The fire effect

Set **effect** to **fire** for a flame that never repeats — great in lanterns, jack-o'-lanterns and torches. The **FIRE** settings shape it:

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **columns**, **height**, **zigzag** | how your strip is arranged — a single strip is 1 column; a strip folded into a grid has several, and *zigzag* means every other row runs backwards |
| **brightness**, **speed** | how bright and how lively |
| **flame**, **noise** | how tall and how ragged the flames are |
| **smoke**, **smoke speed**, **smoke scale** | the darker, drifting layer on top |

In the TEST column a fire strip has **Burn** and **Out**; in a sequence, any value but zero means burning.

<Aside>The fire effect is a port of the "Fire2023" example from the FastLED project.</Aside>
