title: Your first scare in 15 minutes
description: Two boards, one motion sensor, and a pair of glowing eyes that flash when someone walks by.
---

import { Aside, Steps } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

By the end of this page, waving your hand in front of a motion sensor will make a light flash on a **different** board. That is the whole idea of PropMesh, on your desk.

## What you need

- **Two ESP32 DevKit V1 boards** ([why this board](/propmesh/boards/esp32-devkit-v1/)). Other [boards](/propmesh/boards/) work too, but the pin names below are the DevKit's.
- **Two USB cables** that carry data, and a USB phone charger for the second board.
- **A PIR motion sensor.** The common HC-SR501 (a white dome with three pins) is perfect.
- **Three female-to-female jumper wires.**
- A computer with **Chrome or Edge**.

The "glowing eyes" will be the small blue LED that is already on the board, so there is nothing else to wire.

<Steps>

1. ## Install the firmware on both boards

   Follow [Install the firmware](/propmesh/start/flash/) once for each board. It takes about a minute each.

2. ## Wire the motion sensor to one board

   Unplug that board first. With the sensor's dome facing you and its pins at the bottom:

   | Sensor pin | Board pin |
   |---|---|
   | **VCC** (or +) | **VIN** |
   | **OUT** | **D34** |
   | **GND** (or −) | **GND** |

   <Aside>The pin names are printed on the sensor's circuit board, sometimes under the dome. Lift the dome off gently to read them. D34 is a good choice for a sensor because it can only be an input, so it doesn't use up a pin you might want for an output later.</Aside>

   Plug this board into the **charger**. This is your prop board.

3. ## Connect the console

   Plug the **other** board into your computer and open the console:

   **[mrwillson2u.github.io/propmesh/console](/propmesh/console/)**

   In **Nodes & Channels**, press **⌁ Connect USB node** and pick the board from the list. The board appears as a chip at the top of the page.

4. ## Make it the bridge

   Click the board's chip. Type **Bridge** in **Name this node**, switch on the **Bridge** toggle, and press **Save to node**.

   Within a few seconds the prop board appears beside it, first as **connecting…** and then as a chip of its own. The two boards are now talking by radio.

5. ## Tell the prop board what's connected

   Click the prop board's chip and name it **Skeleton**. In its pin table, set:

   | Pin | Function | Name | Options |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **D2** | LED dimmer (PWM) | eyes | — |
   | **D34** | Sensor / button input | gate | pull: **none** |

   Press **Save to node**. Now try it out:
   - Drag the **eyes** slider in the TEST column. The blue LED on the prop board dims and brightens.
   - Wave at the sensor. The **gate** row shows **● ACTIVE**.

   <Aside type="tip" title="The sensor seems stuck on">A PIR sensor needs about a minute to settle after it gets power, and it stays active for a few seconds after it sees motion. The two orange knobs on its back set how far it sees and how long it stays on.</Aside>

6. ## Make a sequence

   Go to **Sequences** and press **+ New sequence**. Name it **Eyes flash**.

   Press **+ Add first track…** and choose **Skeleton → eyes**. Then use **+ Add step** to build this:

   | AT (s) | VALUE |
   |---|---|
   | 0 | 100 % |
   | 0.3 | 0 % |
   | 0.6 | 100 % |
   | 1.5 | 0 % |

   Press **Save to boards**.

7. ## Wire the trigger

   Go to **Show**. Drag a **⊙ Sensor** block onto the graph and set it to **gate**, **becomes active**. Drag a **⚡ Play sequence** block next to it and set it to **Eyes flash**. Drag from the dot on the right side of the sensor block to the dot on the left side of the play block.

   Press **Save triggers**.

8. ## Walk past the sensor

   The mode at the top of **Show** should read **Armed**. A fresh board starts out armed, and stays that way until you set a schedule. If it says something else, press **Armed**.

   Now walk past the sensor. The eyes flash.

</Steps>

## What just happened

The sensor and the light were on the same board this time, but they didn't have to be. The trigger lives on the board with the sensor, and it plays the sequence **on every board at once**. A sensor at the gate can just as easily flash the eyes in a tombstone across the yard. To see it, add a third board, give it an output, and add a track for it to **Eyes flash**.

You can close the console now. The show is stored on the boards and keeps running.

<Aside title="Turning it off at night">
Without a schedule the show stays armed around the clock. Set up a schedule so it arms itself at dusk and goes quiet at bedtime. See [Running the show](/propmesh/show/running/).
</Aside>
